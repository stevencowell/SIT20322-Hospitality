Task 1 · Activity 3

PPE and personal-readiness decisions

Choose when to prepare, stop, report or seek an authorised PPE direction.

20 minutesPairsAutosaves locallyPrint-ready

Supplied context

The scenario

Four learners arrive for different teacher-directed kitchen tasks. Decide the next action without inventing the school's uniform or PPE rules.

Materials

  • This page and the supplied scenario
  • Your saved learning record

Interactive practice

Complete the job

Hair is not controlled and the practical is about to begin.

The chemical label requires eye protection that is not available.

A small waterproof dressing becomes loose during food handling.

A worker reports vomiting before a food-handling shift.

Method

Steps

  1. Select the strongest next action in each case.
  2. Check the consequence-based feedback.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why PPE is the final layer, not a repair for an unsafe process.

Success criteria

  • Pre-start presentation is corrected
  • Missing PPE stops the task
  • Illness and wounds are reported
  • PPE limitations are explained

Support

Ask: can I correct this safely, or does the responsible person need to decide?

Extension

Create one example in which changing the equipment or process is stronger than adding more PPE.

Teacher delivery note

Set-up

The stimulus and core materials are supplied. Substitute a local authorised source only when it materially improves relevance and is safe to share.

Observe

Listen for use of evidence, accurate language, authority boundaries and the learner's explanation—not just the final selection.

Debrief

Ask one group to defend a decision, one group to identify a likely error and one group to state the next source or person to confirm with.
