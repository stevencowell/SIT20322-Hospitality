Task 2 · Lesson 5 of 8

Explain and match products accurately

Develop the product knowledge, value language and verification habits needed to recommend hospitality products without pressure or unsupported claims.

SITXCOM007SITXCCS011
Teach / present this lessonOpen applied activityOpen Menu, customer and complaint cards

Prior learning

Students can distinguish needs, wants and constraints and complete a six-step matching conversation.

Success criteria

  • Identify the product information a worker needs before making a recommendation.
  • Explain how pricing, quality and value contribute to satisfaction without claiming that price alone proves quality.
  • Compare options using confirmed features that connect to the customer's stated need or want.
  • Correct a product-information error promptly and escalate safety-sensitive questions.

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

Product knowledge begins with an authorised source

Product knowledge may include the current name, description, price, portion or service format, availability, quality features, preparation or delivery time where confirmed, and the process for checking ingredients or other safety-sensitive information. The source of that information matters.

Use the current menu, product card, booking information, workplace procedure or authorised person. Memory can become outdated. Promotional wording should not be treated as a complete safety source.

Authentic Example: A menu description says 'seasonal soup'. To answer what today's soup is, the worker checks the current service information rather than assuming yesterday's product is unchanged.
02

Price, quality and value are connected but different

Price is what the customer is charged. Quality is how well the product or service meets the expected standard. Value is the customer's judgement about what they receive compared with the price, need and experience. A higher price does not automatically prove higher quality, and a low price does not excuse inaccurate information or poor presentation.

Fair and transparent pricing supports trust. The product delivered should match the confirmed description and expected standard. If an extra charge or condition applies, it should be explained using the current authorised information before the customer commits.

Worked Example

Customer Need: A light meal within a stated price limit.
Weak Match: Recommend the highest-priced item because it must be best.
Stronger Match: Compare current options that meet the price and light-meal requirements, explain their confirmed features and let the customer choose.
03

Connect a feature to the customer's request

A feature is a factual characteristic of the product or service. A benefit explains why that feature may suit the customer's stated request. The connection must be relevant and honest.

For example, 'This option is currently available in a smaller portion' is a feature. 'That may suit the lighter meal you asked for' connects it to the request. Avoid exaggeration such as 'This is perfect for everyone'.

Feature Benefit Examples

  • Feature: The current practice card lists a non-coffee beverage.
    Benefit Connection: It may suit the customer's stated preference to avoid coffee, subject to any other requirements being checked.
  • Feature: The service format is confirmed as takeaway.
    Benefit Connection: It may suit a customer who has confirmed they cannot remain at the venue.
04

Compare, do not pressure

A recommendation should help the customer decide. Explain two or three relevant options, identify the confirmed differences and connect them to the customer's request. Give the customer space to choose.

Pressure, invented scarcity or unsupported claims may produce a sale but damage trust. Product matching is not the same as pushing the most expensive item.

Workflow

  • Restate the customer's confirmed requirement.
  • Select only options supported by current information.
  • Explain the relevant difference in price, quality feature or service format.
  • State any known limitation.
  • Invite the customer to choose or ask another question.
05

Ingredient and allergy questions need the authorised process

A customer may ask whether a product meets a dietary or allergy requirement. This is not a situation for inference. Record the exact request, use the authorised product and food-safety information, and involve the appropriate person where required.

Do not guarantee 'allergen-free', remove an ingredient and assume the risk is gone, or rely on a generic practice menu. Accurate service includes explaining when further checking is needed.

Source Safe Phrase: I will record that request and check the current authorised product information before confirming an option.
06

Correct misinformation quickly

If a worker realises they gave inaccurate product information, the safest service action is to correct it before the order proceeds where possible. Acknowledge the error, provide the verified information and give the customer an opportunity to reconsider.

Do not hide the mistake because it feels embarrassing. Early correction prevents a larger product, payment, safety or trust problem. Record or escalate the issue if the current procedure requires it.

Worked Example

Situation: A worker described an option as available, then learns it has sold out.
Response: Return promptly, acknowledge the incorrect information, explain the confirmed availability and offer to discuss current alternatives without pressuring the customer.

Suggested sequence

  1. 5 min: retrieval and prior-learning prompt.
  2. 15 min: teach the core explanation and worked example.
  3. 10 min: complete checks with feedback and retry.
  4. 15–20 min: applied activity or teacher-authorised demonstration.
  5. 5 min: written response, evidence destination and exit check.

Likely misconceptions

  • Memory may be outdated and should be checked against current information.
  • Price alone does not establish quality or fit with the customer's need.

Support and adjustment

  • Read the purpose and success criteria aloud; keep both visible.
  • Use the labelled visual before extended reading.
  • Allow paired oral rehearsal before the saved written response.
  • Offer the scaffold without supplying an assessment answer.
  • Use the printable activity for students who need a paper pathway.
