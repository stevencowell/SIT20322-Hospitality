Espresso workflow: confirm, prepare, extract, texture and serve

Sequence espresso production around the confirmed order, current recipe, authorised machine method, observable quality and safe handover.

Prior learning

Recall the previous lesson and bring forward one question or completed learning artefact.

Success criteria

  • I can describe the full espresso workflow without inserting unverified settings or recipe values.
  • I can explain why order confirmation and mise en place occur before extraction begins.
  • I can identify observation and decision points during grinding, extraction and milk texturing.
  • I can sequence service and clean-as-you-go actions that protect quality and teamwork.

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

The workflow begins with the confirmed order

Before touching ingredients or equipment, confirm the beverage, current size or serviceware choice, milk, authorised extras and any verified customer request. Read the current recipe card as a complete sequence. Identify which components can be prepared together and which must wait to protect freshness and quality.

If the order changes, update the authorised record and confirm the change before continuing the affected product. Do not make a familiar version from memory when the current workplace card differs.

AuthenticExample: Two similar milk beverages are ordered with different milk choices. The worker keeps the orders identifiable and confirms each recipe path before grinding or pouring milk.
02

Prepare equipment and serviceware through the authorised method

Complete mise en place so ingredients, clean serviceware, jugs, tools, cloth controls and waste controls are available without crossing another worker's active area. Prepare the machine and grinder according to the current manufacturer and workplace instructions.

Test extraction and adjustment are supervised technical decisions. Observe the current result against the recipe standard and follow only the adjustment process you have been taught and authorised to use. Record or report an unresolved result rather than treating experimentation as service.

Workflow

  • Check current recipe and order.
  • Prepare clean serviceware and station controls.
  • Complete the authorised equipment pre-service process.
  • Observe the teacher-directed test result.
  • Confirm readiness or report the issue.
03

Keep preparation consistent and observable

Select the current beans and grind through the authorised setting and method. Use the required basket and ensure it is prepared as directed. Dose, distribute and tamp to create the even, level coffee bed required by the local process. The exact amount, setting and technique are taught from the current recipe and equipment instructions.

Consistency lets a worker interpret changes. If the grind, dose, distribution, tamp or equipment condition changes unpredictably, the result becomes harder to diagnose. Keep the method stable, avoid contaminating the basket or rim, and do not alter multiple variables at once.

AuthenticExample: The previous result was within the current standard, but the next preparation looks visibly uneven before extraction. The worker corrects the preparation through the taught method rather than hoping extraction will hide it.
04

Extraction is both an action and an observation

Attach and operate the group handle only through the demonstrated sequence. Observe the extraction against the current recipe and quality indicators. Useful observations can include the start and pattern of flow, changes in colour or texture, crema, the used coffee cake and the machine's normal operation.

Describe what happened before naming a cause. If the result differs from the current standard, use only the authorised correction or remake decision. Unexpected machine behaviour, unresolved quality problems or anything outside your training is reported.

Workflow

  • Prepare the current serviceware.
  • Complete the authorised group-head and handle sequence.
  • Start extraction through the taught control.
  • Observe the product and equipment.
  • Accept, adjust, remake or report through the current standard.
05

Milk texturing follows the order and the local product standard

Select the correct milk and suitable clean jug for the order. Follow the authorised steam-wand preparation and hygiene sequence. Texture while monitoring the visual and audible cues taught for the current milk and beverage, then combine the milk and foam as directed and pour without unnecessary delay.

Different milks can behave differently and may have different ingredient or handling requirements. Use the current product and recipe information. Do not top up a jug with a different milk, return used milk to stock or promise that a shared process is suitable for an allergy unless the workplace procedure specifically supports it.

AuthenticExample: The docket and jug label do not match. The worker stops before steaming, verifies the order and replaces the setup through the authorised process rather than guessing which label is wrong.
06

Finish with verification, prompt service and clean-as-you-go

Compare the finished beverage with the order and current standard. Check product, modification, serviceware, appearance, spills, accompaniments and customer or order identifier. Serve promptly through the agreed handover and confirm the affected teammate receives any change or delay information.

Clean as you go using the correct cloth and authorised method. Keep the steam wand, group area, grinder and work surface controlled as directed, remove residue before it affects later products, and avoid unnecessary water, energy and ingredient waste. End-of-service cleaning and filtration work follow manufacturer and workplace instructions.

Workflow

  • Verify product and presentation.
  • Call or identify the order accurately.
  • Handover without touching food-contact surfaces.
  • Complete immediate clean-as-you-go actions.
  • Update stock, faults, waste or handover information.

  3. 10 min: complete checks with feedback and retry.
  4. 15–20 min: applied activity or teacher-authorised demonstration.
  5. 5 min: written response, evidence destination and exit check.

Likely misconceptions

  • Not yet. Memory and the active record now disagree, creating a predictable handover error.
  • Not yet. Multiple simultaneous changes make it difficult to connect any adjustment with the result and may exceed authority.

Support and adjustment

  • Read the purpose and success criteria aloud; keep both visible.
  • Use the labelled visual before extended reading.
  • Allow paired oral rehearsal before the saved written response.
  • Offer the scaffold without supplying an assessment answer.
  • Use the printable activity for students who need a paper pathway.
