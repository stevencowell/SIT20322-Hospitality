Storage and temperature

Receive, label, separate, rotate and monitor food in the authorised storage environment, using current temperature controls and honest corrective action without inventing numerical limits.

Prior learning

Success criteria

  • Links hazard, control, monitoring and corrective action
  • Keeps failed food out of use
  • Uses the actual scenario evidence
  • Does not invent a temperature, date mark or local rejection procedure

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

Move accepted food into control promptly

Storage control begins at receiving. Compare the item with the authorised order and acceptance criteria, complete any required condition or measurement check using the approved method, and keep failed or uncertain products outside usable stock.

Accepted food should move promptly to the correct authorised storage area. Leaving it in a delivery or preparation zone can expose it to time, temperature, pests, damage, confusion or contamination before the planned storage controls begin.

02

Protect identity and traceability

Keep food in the approved container or transfer it only through the authorised process. Labels and date information must identify the real product and history required by the workplace. Never create a new date or description to make an unknown item appear usable.

Protect packaging and labels from damage and keep stock records accurate. If identity, source, date, allergen information or storage history is missing or unreliable, isolate the food and ask the responsible person to apply the current procedure.

03

Separate food and non-food hazards

Arrange stock in the designated storage zones so raw products cannot contaminate ready-to-eat food. Protect open and prepared food, separate allergens as directed, and keep chemicals, personal items and cleaning equipment out of food storage areas.

Do not overload shelves, block airflow or place heavy and leaking items where they may endanger people or other stock. Use stable containers and the authorised shelf arrangement. Report damage, spills, pests or evidence that separation has failed.

04

Rotate stock using the authorised date system

Use the workplace stock-rotation system so suitable older stock is considered before later stock while product requirements and date information remain controlling. Rotation is not permission to use an item that has failed a condition, date, identity or storage check.

Place stock so labels can be read and items can be inspected without unnecessary handling. During each access, look for damage, leakage, pests, changed condition or an item in the wrong zone and respond before adding more stock around it.

05

Monitor time and temperature using current limits

Use the current authorised limits and monitoring points for receiving, storage, preparation, holding and service. Select the approved measuring device, check it as directed, measure the correct product or storage point and record the result when the check occurs.

This module deliberately does not state universal numerical limits. Current figures, device checks and time rules must come from the current approved guidance and workplace procedure used by the teacher. Never copy an old workbook figure into a live record without confirmation.

06

Isolate, correct, report and record

When identity, separation, date, time or temperature control fails, keep the affected item from use and notify the responsible person. Follow the authorised corrective action, which may include rejecting, isolating, moving, adjusting, discarding or further reviewing the food according to the real conditions.

Record the actual observation or measurement, product, location, action and notification as required. Do not invent a pass result, backfill a check that did not occur or decide alone that rapid use will make failed food acceptable.

WorkedExample: Observed evidence: During a fictional storage check, a learner finds a prepared container beside correctly identified stock, but this container has no reliable label and the current monitoring record cannot be matched to it. Decision and reason: They decide that neither identity nor control history can be verified, so inventing a date, temperature or intended use would create false evidence. Safe action: The learner isolates the container from use, maintains separation and reports its location and condition to the responsible teacher. Result: It remains unavailable while the authorised person determines the disposition. Next check or record: The learner checks nearby labels and records, corrects nothing unless directed, and documents the observed facts, isolation and notification through the authorised system.

Likely misconceptions

  • Using it quickly does not restore a failed receiving control or establish that it is safe.
  • This describes identity, protection and separation controls working together.

Support and adjustment

  • Read the purpose and success criteria aloud; keep both visible.
  • Use the labelled visual before extended reading.
  • Allow paired oral rehearsal before the saved written response.
  • Offer the scaffold without supplying an assessment answer.
