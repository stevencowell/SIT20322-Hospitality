Build, share and improve an industry brief

Synthesise current hospitality information into a concise, traceable briefing that a colleague can use, then test understanding and improve the recommendation.

SITHIND006
Teach / present this lessonOpen applied activityOpen Two-source industry brief

Prior learning

Recall the previous lesson and bring forward one question or completed learning artefact.

Success criteria

  • I can build a brief around a focused question, audience and workplace purpose.
  • I can synthesise complementary sources while preserving differences, dates and limitations.
  • I can recommend a realistic action that follows from the evidence and stays within authority.
  • I can share information, use open and closed probe questions and improve the brief from feedback.

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

A brief is written for someone to use

An industry brief is shorter than a research report but still traceable. It tells a named audience what they need to know, why it matters for their work and what next step the evidence supports. The opening should state the focused question, role or context and decision without making the reader search for the purpose.

Audience changes emphasis, not accuracy. A new worker may need plain language and a clear local confirmation point. A supervisor may need the evidence difference, operational effect and decision required. Both need sources and limitations that can be checked.

AuthenticExample: A brief about a fictional booking-system change begins: This note helps reception and function staff understand how the proposed update could change booking handovers. It does not begin with a history of hospitality technology.
02

Give each source a job in the evidence matrix

A defensible learning brief uses multiple source types and records what each contributes. The current unit's performance evidence includes sourcing and documenting industry information using at least three information sources. This package uses a three-source practice pattern, while the current authorised assessment package controls the formal evidence students must submit.

Build a matrix with source authority, status or period, relevant finding, contribution and limitation. One source may establish a requirement, another current industry context and another workplace application. Do not count duplicate copies or several pages from the same unsupported claim as independent confirmation.

Workflow

  • Place the focused question above the matrix.
  • Record organisation, title, link and current status for each source.
  • Summarise the exact finding in your own words.
  • State the different job each source performs in the brief.
  • Record a limitation or unresolved difference for every important claim.
03

Synthesis explains the relationship between findings

A source-by-source list makes the reader do the thinking. Synthesis groups evidence around the workplace question. It shows where sources agree, where they address different parts of the issue and where their scope prevents a direct comparison.

Use linking language carefully: both sources indicate, the regulator establishes while the industry profile provides context, or the workplace source applies the public guidance locally. If a source conflicts, explain why or name the unresolved issue. Never change a number or remove a limitation to make the paragraph sound smoother.

AuthenticExample: The official technology list shows that reservation and tracking systems are relevant industry categories. A cyber-security source identifies customer-data considerations. The fictional workplace scenario then shows which reception handover would change. Together they support a workflow review, not an automatic purchase.
04

The recommendation must follow from the evidence

A useful application names the affected duty, skill, product, service, supplier relationship or team process. The recommendation states who could take the next action, what is within that role and what requires approval. It also includes a way to check whether the action improves performance.

Avoid absolute language unless the responsible source uses it for the same scope. A brief can recommend checking, briefing, trialling, updating, asking or escalating. It cannot invent a workplace rule, declare legal compliance, approve a purchase or promise a result.

Workflow

  • Name the finding most relevant to the workplace question.
  • Identify the role, service or process affected.
  • Write one action that the evidence supports.
  • Add the responsible role and any approval or clarification point.
  • Choose observable evidence for checking the result.
05

Sharing includes checking what the colleague understood

Information is not shared effectively just because it was spoken or sent. A short verbal briefing should state the question, key finding, limitation and action, then invite questions. Open probe questions such as what part affects your role invite explanation. Closed probe questions such as which source controls the next step confirm a specific detail.

The presenter listens for misunderstanding, uncertainty and workplace knowledge that changes the application. They do not pressure a colleague to agree. If a question cannot be answered from the sources, it becomes a follow-up item with an owner and source route.

AuthenticExample: After a briefing, a colleague asks whether the recommendation applies to a different venue type. The presenter does not guess. They state the current source boundary and record the venue-coverage question for checking.
06

Feedback improves the evidence trail, not just the wording

Useful feedback identifies what was clear, what could not be traced, where the scope was too broad and whether the recommendation could be acted on. Revise the evidence matrix or limitation when needed, not only the formatting. Keep a simple note of the change and reason.

Before sharing the final version, run a privacy and source-boundary check. Remove real people, customer data, student work, protected workplace records and unsupported local claims. Confirm that links work, reference periods are visible and the recommendation still matches the finding.

Workflow

  • Ask the colleague to state the key finding in their own words.
  • Record one evidence question and one application question.
  • Check the source or limitation instead of answering from memory.
  • Revise the brief and note what changed.
  • Complete source, privacy and authority checks before final sharing.

Written application

Explain the idea in context

Saved automatically on this device0 words
  1. Question and audience: This brief helps... decide or understand...
  2. Source trail: The official source establishes...; the industry source adds...; the workplace or training source applies...
  3. Synthesis: Together the sources indicate..., while they differ or are limited by...
  4. Workplace effect: For the role or service, this changes...
  5. Recommendation: The responsible role could..., after confirming...
  6. Review: The team would look for...
  7. Follow-up: We still need to check...

Write one connected argument rather than three source summaries. Keep dates, scope and limitations visible and remove all real customer, worker, student and private workplace information.
