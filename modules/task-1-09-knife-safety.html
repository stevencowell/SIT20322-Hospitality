Knife safety

Select and handle kitchen knives through a stable work zone, authorised cutting demonstration, controlled transport, washing and storage without teaching unsupervised sharpening.

Success criteria

  • Matches the knife and board to the nominated task

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

Match the knife to the authorised cutting task

Different knife designs support different food-preparation actions. Use the knife named or demonstrated for the teacher-authorised task and confirm unfamiliar tools before handling them. Consider the food's size, structure and stability rather than choosing a knife because it looks large or familiar.

A suitable knife does not make an unsafe technique acceptable. Selection must be combined with a stable board, clear work zone, controlled food position and the cutting method taught for that task.

02

Inspect condition without attempting maintenance

Before use, check that the handle is secure, the blade is undamaged and clean, and the knife is in the condition required for controlled cutting. A damaged, loose or unsuitable knife must be kept from use and reported.

Knife sharpening and maintenance require the approved equipment, method, training and supervision. This lesson deliberately provides no sharpening angle or step-by-step sharpening instruction. An online video may support observation, but it does not authorise a learner to sharpen a knife.

WorkedExample: Observed evidence: Before a fictional produce task, the learner feels movement between a knife handle and blade during the condition check. No cutting has begun. Decision and reason: They decide the knife is unsuitable because a loose handle can reduce control; using less force or choosing soft food would not remove the defect. Safe action: The learner keeps the knife visible and out of use, reports it and follows the teacher's direction for secure handling of the defective item. They select only a demonstrated replacement. Result: The task proceeds without improvising repair or sharpening. Next check or record: The learner checks the replacement handle, blade, board and work zone, then records the loose-handle observation.
03

Build a stable cutting zone

Use the designated board for the food and prevent it moving through the approved setup. Place the board on a stable, clear bench with enough space for the uncut food, cutting action and finished pieces. Keep the knife away from the bench edge and keep waste from crowding the blade path.

Maintain food-safety separation. Reset the board, knife, hands and work zone when changing between incompatible foods, and protect ready-to-eat ingredients from raw food and dirty equipment.

04

Use only the demonstrated cutting method

Adopt the hand positions, body position and cutting action demonstrated by the teacher for the nominated food. Work at a controlled pace, keep attention on the task and stop when the food becomes unstable, the knife catches or another person enters the cutting space.

Do not cut towards another person, use unnecessary force, attempt tricks or copy a fast technique from entertainment media. Speed develops only after consistent control and is never more important than safe, accurate portions.

05

Carry, place and pass with clear communication

Avoid carrying a knife through a busy area unless the authorised task requires it. Use the demonstrated carrying method, maintain a clear path and communicate so nearby people know you are moving. Place the knife in a visible controlled location when not in hand.

Never leave a knife hidden under food, cloths or washing water. Do not try to catch a dropped knife. Warn others, allow it to fall and restore control of the area before picking it up as directed. Use the authorised passing method rather than handing over an exposed blade unexpectedly.

06

Wash and store so the next person is protected

Keep knives visible during wash-up and use the authorised hand-cleaning method rather than placing them loose into cloudy or crowded water. Clean and sanitise as directed, dry using the safe method and return the knife to its designated rack, sheath or storage location.

Report any damage noticed during use or cleaning. A knife returned loose to a drawer, hidden in a sink or left on a bench transfers the hazard to the next person and fails the final stage of the task.

External media loads only after you choose it.

Likely misconceptions

  • A loose handle can affect control regardless of the food and does not pass the pre-use check.
  • Reaching for a falling blade creates an unpredictable cutting path.

Support and adjustment

  • Read the purpose and success criteria aloud; keep both visible.
  • Use the labelled visual before extended reading.
  • Allow paired oral rehearsal before the saved written response.
  • Offer the scaffold without supplying an assessment answer.
  • Use the printable activity for students who need a paper pathway.
