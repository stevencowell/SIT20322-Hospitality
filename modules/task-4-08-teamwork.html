Task 4 · Lesson 8 of 8

Teamwork: share information, support service and close the loop

Work cooperatively through preparation, service and end-of-shift duties by clarifying responsibilities, sharing timely information, using feedback and escalating issues respectfully.

Teach / present this lessonOpen applied activityOpen Closed-loop handover sheet

Prior learning

Recall the previous lesson and bring forward one question or completed learning artefact.

Success criteria

  • I can distinguish my responsibility from the workgroup goal and identify when information must be shared.
  • I can use a closed-loop handover for a change, delay, fault or customer request.
  • I can support a team member without abandoning my own role or taking an unauthorised task.
  • I can respond to feedback, communication differences and routine conflict respectfully and seek supervisor help when needed.

Learning boundary

This lesson supports preparation and formative practice. The current authorised package and trainer/assessor control formal products, observations, dates and submission.

01

Clear roles support one shared service goal

A workgroup combines different responsibilities to achieve an outcome none of the roles can deliver alone. Front of house, beverage preparation, food production, clearing and supervision may each control different actions and information. Know your own responsibility, who depends on your output and who must be told when conditions change.

Teamwork does not mean everyone does everything. It means responsibilities are clear, information crosses role boundaries and people support the shared goal without working outside training or authority. When ownership is uncertain, clarify it before the task is missed or duplicated.

AuthenticExample: Front of house changes a fictional drink order. The beverage worker owns the product change, the service worker owns the customer update, and both rely on the authorised order record.
02

Choose the approved communication channel

Hospitality teams may use briefings, dockets, ordering technology, verbal calls, one-to-one updates, meetings or supervisor directions. The best channel depends on urgency, the information required, who must act and the workplace procedure. A safety issue may require immediate verbal escalation as well as a formal record; a routine stock update may use the normal handover process.

Use concise, specific language and confirm understanding. Avoid broadcasting private customer information, relying on unofficial side messages or assuming silence means agreement. When the system fails, use the authorised fallback and make sure the active source of truth is updated.

Workflow

  • Identify what changed and how urgent it is.
  • Select the approved channel.
  • State the affected task or order and required action.
  • Ask the receiver to confirm.
  • Update the record and check the result.
03

A handover finishes with ownership and confirmation

A complete handover states what changed, which order or task is affected, what has already been done, who owns the next action and when or how the result will be checked. The receiver confirms the message. The sender remains responsible for any customer or record update that belongs to their role.

Vague language creates hidden work. Something changed at table four does not identify the product, the customer request, the active record or the next owner. Specific language reduces the need for repeated questions and protects timing, safety and quality.

AuthenticExample: Table four changed the second latte to the authorised alternative milk. I updated the docket. Can you confirm you have it? I will advise the customer of the revised timing and check the handover.
04

Support the team without creating a second gap

Notice workload, delays and blocked tasks early. Offer specific support, share relevant information and ask what would help the team goal. Before changing tasks, confirm who will cover your current responsibility and whether you are trained and authorised for the requested work.

Effective support can include restocking through the agreed system, preparing approved serviceware, relaying information, clearing a safe work path or seeking supervisor help. Taking over unfamiliar equipment or silently abandoning an order is not support.

AuthenticExample: A teammate is managing several orders while clean serviceware is low. Instead of using unfamiliar equipment, the worker confirms priorities and completes the approved replenishment task while keeping their own active order covered.
05

Use feedback and communication differences respectfully

Ask for specific feedback on an observable action, listen without interrupting, clarify what is required and apply the agreed improvement. Feedback is useful when it connects behaviour with service effect. Record the next action rather than defending an intention the observer could not see.

Team members may use different language, pace, tone or communication styles. Avoid stereotypes. Use plain language, check understanding and adapt within workplace and ethical standards. Personal values must not become discrimination, bullying or disrespect. Seek supervisor support for persistent communication barriers or inappropriate behaviour.

Workflow

  • Listen to the specific feedback or concern.
  • Clarify the behaviour and expected change.
  • Acknowledge the service effect.
  • Apply the agreed change.
  • Check whether the result improved.
06

Address issues and finish the shift with usable information

For a routine issue, state the facts, describe the work effect, listen to the other person and suggest a procedure-based next step. Keep the focus on the task rather than attacking the person. Threats, harassment, discrimination, unsafe conduct, repeated unresolved conflict or matters outside your authority go to the supervisor or other authorised person.

End-of-shift teamwork includes completing the authorised cleaning, storage and administration, reporting faults and shortages, sharing customer feedback, and handing over unfinished work. A useful handover gives the next team a truthful starting point; it does not hide waste, damage or a missed task to protect appearances.

AuthenticExample: A recurring order delay is discussed at handover using the affected stage and observable pattern. The team suggests checking the information flow next service instead of blaming one person without evidence.
