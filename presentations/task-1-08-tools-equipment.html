Task 1 · Lesson 8

Tools and equipment

Select, inspect, set up, operate, clean and store kitchen tools and equipment within the authorised demonstration, manufacturer information and learner's training.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Matches equipment to the teacher-nominated task
  • Includes condition, guards and setup checks
  • Separates authorised operation from fault response
  • Does not invent a maintenance or repair procedure

Visual explanation

Select to store: the safe equipment loop

A seven-stage ring shows Select, Inspect, Set up, Operate, Stop, Clean and Store. Fault icons at inspection and operation exit to Keep from use and Report rather than re-entering the loop. Energy, guard, water and work-zone symbols provide visual checkpoints.

Worked example

Inspect before setup

Check that the item is clean, complete and in suitable condition. Look for cracks, loose handles, damaged blades, missing guards, unstable feet, frayed leads, damaged plugs, leaks or controls that do not operate as demonstrated. For electrical equipment, keep the item disconnected while assembling it if the authorised method requires this.

Observed evidence: During a fictional pre-use check, a learner finds that an appliance guard does not seat as it did in the authorised demonstration. The machine has not been powered. Decision and reason: They decide the incomplete guard makes the equipment unsuitable and that pushing, taping or testing it would become an unauthorised repair or operation. Safe action: The learner leaves the appliance unpowered, prevents it being included in the station and reports the exact fit problem to the teacher. Result: An authorised alternative is selected and the production plan is adjusted. Next check or record: The learner inspects the replacement, confirms its demonstrated setup and records the original observable defect for the next user.

Question the room

What should control the selection of kitchen equipment?

  1. The authorised method, food quantity, required result and worker's training.
  2. Whichever appliance appears fastest, regardless of capacity or training.
  3. The item that another group has already collected.

Apply

Hazard, risk or control?

A café team is reviewing a pre-service inspection. Classify each statement before proposing one stronger control.

  1. Classify all six statements.
  2. Check the result and read the explanation for any mismatch.
  3. Choose one control and explain why it is stronger than only warning people.
