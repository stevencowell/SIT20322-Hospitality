Task 1 · Lesson 5

Handwashing and contamination

Trace contamination through hands, gloves and high-touch surfaces, recognise handwashing triggers and restore a clean ready-to-eat workflow after contact changes.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Names a source, route and food at risk
  • Explains rather than merely states wash hands
  • Includes clean equipment and separation
  • Does not invent a local time, product or procedure

Core ideas

Build the complete picture

1

Hands are a moving contact surface

2

Recognise the trigger before the next touch

3

Handwashing breaks a transfer route

4

Gloves are a controlled surface, not a shortcut

Visual explanation

How a clean hand or glove becomes contaminated again

A central hand-and-glove illustration connects common touchpoints such as raw food, face and hair, phone, waste, cleaning and money to a ready-to-eat product. Break points show report, wash and dry, change gloves, replace equipment and separate the work zone.

Worked example

Reset the whole route after an accidental touch

If hands or gloves touch the face, hair, phone, waste, dirty cloth or another contamination source, stop the food task. Remove or replace contaminated single-use items, wash and dry hands using the authorised method and restore clean equipment and the protected zone before continuing.

Observed evidence: In a fictional food-preparation situation, a learner washes and dries their hands, then silences a personal phone and touches the handle of a clean utensil before reaching towards ready-to-eat food. Decision and reason: They recognise that the phone created a new transfer route; clean hands alone no longer protect the utensil or food. Safe action: The learner stops before touching the food, stores the phone away, tells the teacher what was contacted, repeats the authorised handwashing process and resets the utensil as directed. Result: The contamination pathway is broken before production resumes. Next check or record: They trace each contact from phone to hand to handle, check the protected zone and record why the reset was required.

Question the room

After handling raw food, a worker must prepare a ready-to-eat item. What comes first?

  1. Put a clean glove over the contaminated glove.
  2. Stop, remove contamination, wash and dry hands correctly, then use clean equipment in the separated ready-to-eat zone.
  3. Wipe hands or gloves on the apron and continue.

Apply

Rebuild the handwashing control

A learner has moved from handling raw food to a ready-to-eat preparation zone. The handwashing steps have been mixed up.

  1. Move the seven steps into a defensible order.
  2. Check the sequence.
  3. Explain which step is defeated if clean hands touch a contaminated tap, apron or phone.
