Task 2 · Lesson 4

Match needs and wants through questions

Distinguish requirements from preferences and use a six-step, source-safe conversation to match an authorised product or service.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Explain the difference between a need, a want and a service constraint.
  • Use open and focused questions to gather relevant information.
  • Follow the six-step matching conversation and close the communication loop.
  • Recognise when a request requires authorised product, food-safety or policy information.

Match needs and wants through questions

Worked example

Listening is demonstrated by confirmation

Active listening is more than staying quiet. A worker shows listening by identifying key words, asking about ambiguity and repeating the important requirement in their own words. Confirmation gives the customer a chance to correct the record before production or booking begins.

Customer: 'I have ten minutes and I don't want coffee.' Worker: 'You need an option that can be provided within the confirmed time, and you would prefer a non-coffee drink. Is there anything else I should check?'

Question the room

A customer says, 'I must leave in fifteen minutes, but I would prefer something warm.' Which statement is accurate?

  1. The time is a constraint and warm is a preference that should be matched using confirmed options.
  2. Warm is the only need, so timing can be ignored.
  3. The worker should choose the fastest item without asking anything else.

Apply

Customer profiles and needs

The venue is a fictional daytime café with step-free entry, dine-in and takeaway service, a simple menu and staff who can check ingredient information. Do not assume a need that the customer has not expressed.

  1. Choose the best response for each stated need.
  2. Check the explanations.
  3. Write one open question and one closed confirmation question for a selected profile.
