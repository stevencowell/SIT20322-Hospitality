Task 1 · Lesson 10

Recipe and yield

Read a standard recipe as a production specification, interpret units and yield, calculate authorised quantity changes and test whether the result remains workable and safe.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Uses an authorised recipe rather than inventing ingredients
  • Shows the yield relationship and units
  • Checks capacity, batches, timing and method
  • Connects planned quantities to waste prevention

Worked example

Scaling is not only multiplying ingredients

A larger or smaller yield may change equipment capacity, batch size, preparation space, timing and service sequence. Some ingredients or processes may require special handling rather than a simple mathematical adjustment. Follow the authorised recipe and seek teacher confirmation where the scaled process is unclear.

Observed evidence: A fictional service request requires a different output from the yield stated on the current standard recipe, and the available mixing bowl appears too small for one enlarged batch. Decision and reason: The learner decides that multiplying ingredients alone would ignore equipment capacity and process sequence. Safe action: They calculate a proposed scale factor on the planning sheet, keep every unit and ingredient relationship aligned, then ask the teacher to confirm batch division, equipment and any method change. Result: An authorised production plan is agreed before ingredients are issued. Next check or record: The learner recalculates total planned output, checks that each batch follows the current recipe and records only the confirmed adjustments.

Question the room

Which information must be checked before scaling a recipe?

  1. Only the name of the product.
  2. Original yield, required yield, ingredient quantities and units, plus any process or capacity limits.
  3. The amount another class happened to prepare.

Apply

Sandwich production and waste sequence

Brief: prepare four consistent ready-to-eat salad sandwiches using the teacher-authorised recipe. The exact ingredients, quantities and local presentation standard remain in that recipe.

  1. Order all nine stages.
  2. Check the sequence.
  3. Name one waste-prevention decision and one food-safety control that must operate together.
