Task 1 · Lesson 7

Cleaning and sanitising

Distinguish cleaning from sanitising, follow the authorised sequence and use chemical information without guessing dilution, contact time or compatibility.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Distinguishes cleaning from sanitising
  • Uses the label, SDS and authorised procedure as controlling sources
  • Explains separation of clean and dirty stages
  • Does not invent dilution, contact time or local sign-off

Core ideas

Build the complete picture

1

Cleaning and sanitising solve different problems

2

Start with the item and contamination risk

3

4

Visual explanation

Worked example

Work from dirty to controlled without recontamination

Organise the sequence so dirty items do not cross back over cleaned items. Remove waste and loose soil, process equipment in the authorised order, keep clean and dirty tools separated and change cloths or solutions as directed. Hands and gloves may also need to be reset when moving between stages.

Observed evidence: A fictional preparation board still has visible food residue, while an unlabelled spray bottle has been left beside the sink. Decision and reason: The learner decides that spraying over residue will not complete cleaning and that an unknown chemical cannot be used safely. Safe action: They remove the board from food use, protect nearby ingredients and report the bottle. Following teacher direction, they use only the approved labelled product and authorised clean-then-sanitise sequence. Result: Dirty and controlled items remain separated and the board returns only after the required verification. Next check or record: The learner checks the transfer path, names the controlling label or procedure and records completion without inventing dilution, contact time or rinse directions.

Question the room

A bench looks clean after wiping. Why might it still be unsafe for food preparation?

  1. Visible soil may be gone, but the approved sanitising step may not have occurred.
  2. A bench can only become safe after it has been left overnight.
  3. Sanitiser works best when visible food remains on the surface.

Apply

Clean, sanitise or unsafe?

A closing team has recorded six actions. Classify what each action actually achieves.

  1. Classify all actions.
  2. Check the result.
  3. Write the source you must use for product, dilution, PPE, contact time and rinse directions.
