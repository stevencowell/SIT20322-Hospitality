Task 1 · Lesson 3

Hazards and controls

Recognise common hospitality hazards, reason from hazard to effective control and choose an immediate response that stays within training and workplace authority.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Identifies more than one interacting hazard
  • Separates immediate protection from authorised corrective action
  • Includes factual reporting details
  • Avoids inventing a chemical, repair or local emergency procedure

Worked example

Act immediately, but stay within your authority

When you find a hazard, protect people from immediate exposure if you can do so safely. Warn nearby people, stop the affected task, keep the item or area from use and notify the responsible person. Isolation, tagging, clean-up, first aid or equipment shutdown must follow the training and procedure you have been given.

Observed evidence: In a fictional storeroom setup, a delivery crate narrows the doorway and a small unknown liquid patch is visible beside it. Decision and reason: The learner identifies both an obstructed movement route and an uncertain spill; moving the crate alone would not control the whole situation. Safe action: They stop others entering, do not touch the liquid and report the location, appearance and blocked path to the responsible teacher. Result: The area stays isolated while an authorised person identifies the substance and directs the response. Next check or record: After clearance, the learner checks the full doorway and nearby floor, then records the hazards, immediate protection and person notified without guessing the cause.

Question the room

Which statement best distinguishes a hazard from risk?

  1. A hazard can cause harm; risk considers the possible harm and likelihood under the current conditions.
  2. A hazard is serious, while a risk is always minor.
  3. A hazard exists only after someone is injured.

Apply

Hazard, risk or control?

A café team is reviewing a pre-service inspection. Classify each statement before proposing one stronger control.

  1. Classify all six statements.
  2. Check the result and read the explanation for any mismatch.
  3. Choose one control and explain why it is stronger than only warning people.
