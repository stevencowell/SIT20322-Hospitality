Task 4 · Lesson 5

Espresso foundations: product, equipment and customer language

Build the vocabulary and product knowledge needed to understand espresso orders, recognise workstation components and use the current recipe and equipment instructions responsibly.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • I can describe how bean type, blend, roast, freshness and grind relate to the final product without inventing a recipe.
  • I can identify the main espresso workstation components and state their general purpose.
  • I can organise common espresso beverages into useful families while recognising that local recipes control exact construction.
  • I can clarify customer preferences and refer to verified ingredient information.

Worked example

Beans, blends and roasts shape the starting material

The authorised teacher terminology identifies Arabica and Robusta as major commercial coffee species. A blend may combine coffees to create a target balance of aroma, body, flavour and consistency. Roasting applies heat to green beans and develops the characteristics used in the final beverage.

Two products both described as medium roast may use different beans and blend goals. The current product description, not the shared roast label alone, supports an accurate recommendation.

Question the room

Two coffee products share the same roast description but have different blend information. What is the strongest advice approach?

  1. Tell the customer they will taste identical because the roast label matches.
  2. Use the current verified product descriptions and explain only the characteristics they support.
  3. Recommend the more expensive product as automatically stronger.
