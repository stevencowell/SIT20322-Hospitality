Roles, careers and work ethic

Investigate hospitality roles through duties, conditions, pathways, work ethic and authoritative employment information without turning a career snapshot into personal or legal advice.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • I can compare roles using duties, responsibilities, work context and transferable skills.
  • I can describe work ethic through observable behaviour rather than personality labels.
  • I can choose suitable official sources for training, occupation and employment-condition questions.
  • I can explain why merit, equality and role boundaries matter in a hospitality workplace.

Worked example

Look past the job title

A job title is a starting point, not a complete role description. The same title can involve different duties in a café, hotel, club, function venue or catering operation. Compare the products or services supported, customer contact, equipment or systems used, team relationships, supervision and decisions within the role.

Two workers are both called food and beverage attendants. In one fictional venue the role centres on counter orders and table clearing; in another it supports functions, setup and coordinated table service. The title alone cannot establish the exact duties.

Question the room

Which role snapshot provides the strongest evidence?

  1. Baristas are friendly and chefs are creative.
  2. The role supports the service by completing named duties, using specified information and handing changes to the responsible role.
  3. Every worker with the same title performs identical duties.

Apply

Source credibility matrix

Source A is a current training.gov.au unit page that identifies its code, release, application and evidence requirements. Source B is an undated social post saying 'every venue does it this way' with no author, workplace context or supporting link.

  1. Compare both supplied source descriptions.
  2. Rate authority, currency, relevance and evidence.
  3. Write the workplace decision each source can and cannot support.
  4. Record a second independent source you would seek.

Speaker notes and delivery prompts
  1. Open with this purpose: Investigate hospitality roles through duties, conditions, pathways, work ethic and authoritative employment information without turning a career snapshot into personal or legal advice.
  2. Use Look past the job title to connect prior learning to the first observable workplace decision.
  3. Model Look past the job title by walking through this supplied example: Two workers are both called food and beverage attendants. In one fictional venue the role centres on counter orders and table clearing; in another it supports functions, setup and coordinated table service. The title alone cannot establish the exact duties.
  4. Ask: Which role snapshot provides the strongest evidence? Confirm the strongest response as The role supports the service by completing named duties, using specified information and handing changes to the responsible role..
  5. Listen for this misconception: Not yet. These are stereotypes and do not identify duties, context, authority or evidence.
  6. Move into Source credibility matrix, then collect the saved response and finish with the source boundary: Authorised source note: Career opportunities, general role responsibilities, work ethic, industrial-relations concepts and equal employment opportunity are source-supported knowledge domains.
