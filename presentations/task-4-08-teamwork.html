Task 4 · Lesson 8

Teamwork: share information, support service and close the loop

Work cooperatively through preparation, service and end-of-shift duties by clarifying responsibilities, sharing timely information, using feedback and escalating issues respectfully.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • I can distinguish my responsibility from the workgroup goal and identify when information must be shared.
  • I can use a closed-loop handover for a change, delay, fault or customer request.
  • I can support a team member without abandoning my own role or taking an unauthorised task.
  • I can respond to feedback, communication differences and routine conflict respectfully and seek supervisor help when needed.

Worked example

Clear roles support one shared service goal

A workgroup combines different responsibilities to achieve an outcome none of the roles can deliver alone. Front of house, beverage preparation, food production, clearing and supervision may each control different actions and information. Know your own responsibility, who depends on your output and who must be told when conditions change.

Front of house changes a fictional drink order. The beverage worker owns the product change, the service worker owns the customer update, and both rely on the authorised order record.

Question the room

Which handover is most useful after a customer changes one drink?

  1. Something changed at table four.
  2. Table four changed the second latte to the authorised alternative milk. The docket is updated; please confirm receipt while I update the customer.
  3. The customer was difficult, so check every drink again.

Apply

Closed-loop team handover

Handover stimulus: Table 6 is waiting for two hot drinks. One customer requested a non-dairy option; the authorised product has been confirmed and the cups are ready. The espresso machine has just shown an unfamiliar warning. No drink has been started. The supervisor needs to be told before equipment use continues.

  1. Build a concise handover from the stimulus.
  2. Deliver it to a partner.
  3. Receiver repeats the order, constraint and next action.
  4. Observer checks the nine fields and records one improvement.

Speaker notes and delivery prompts
  1. Open with this purpose: Work cooperatively through preparation, service and end-of-shift duties by clarifying responsibilities, sharing timely information, using feedback and escalating issues respectfully.
  2. Use Clear roles support one shared service goal to connect prior learning to the first observable workplace decision.
  3. Model Clear roles support one shared service goal by walking through this supplied example: Front of house changes a fictional drink order. The beverage worker owns the product change, the service worker owns the customer update, and both rely on the authorised order record.
  4. Ask: Which handover is most useful after a customer changes one drink? Confirm the strongest response as Table four changed the second latte to the authorised alternative milk. The docket is updated; please confirm receipt while I update the customer..
  5. Listen for this misconception: Not yet. The message does not identify the item, change, active record, owner or required action.
