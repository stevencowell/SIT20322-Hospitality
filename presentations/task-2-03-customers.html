Worked example

Customer categories describe context, not personality

Hospitality businesses may describe customer contexts such as leisure, business, group, local, international and repeat customers. These categories can help plan staffing, information, space and product ranges. They do not tell a worker exactly what an individual will want.

Instead of saying 'Business customers always want fast service', ask 'Do you have a time you need to leave by?'

Question the room

What is the most appropriate use of a customer profile?

  1. Use patterns to plan likely options and questions, then confirm the individual customer's needs.
  2. Decide what every person in the group will prefer.
  3. Collect any personal information that might be interesting.

Apply

Customer profiles and needs

The venue is a fictional daytime café with step-free entry, dine-in and takeaway service, a simple menu and staff who can check ingredient information. Do not assume a need that the customer has not expressed.

  1. Choose the best response for each stated need.
  2. Check the explanations.
  3. Write one open question and one closed confirmation question for a selected profile.
