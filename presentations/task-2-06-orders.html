Take bookings and orders with a closed communication loop

Collect, record, repeat and hand over the details needed for accurate bookings and food or beverage orders while protecting personal information.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Identify the fields supplied by the authorised booking worksheet and explain why they need accurate recording.
  • Use a prepare-greet-listen-record-clarify-repeat-submit order sequence.
  • Record modifications and safety-sensitive requests without shortening away meaning.
  • Protect customer information and use only fictional data in course-site practice.

Worked example

Closed-loop handover prevents silent errors

The order is not complete when the worker stops writing. The information must reach the correct work area in a usable form. Closed-loop communication means the sender records or states the message, the receiver can access or acknowledge it, and uncertainty is clarified before production continues.

The kitchen queries an unreadable modification. The floor worker checks the original request and customer confirmation, then updates the authorised record. Guessing is not a handover.

Question the room

Which booking details should be repeated before the interaction closes?

  1. Only the guest's first name.
  2. The important recorded details, including date, time, guest number and relevant requests.
  3. Nothing, because repeating details wastes time.

Apply

Booking and order accuracy check

Incoming call: 'Sam Lee, table for four next Friday at six. One guest needs step-free access. Please call me on 0400 555 018 if there is a problem.' Draft record: Name—Sam; People—4; Time—6; Note—access; Contact—blank.

  1. Compare the call with the draft record.
  2. Tick every field that is incomplete or ambiguous.
  3. Write a concise read-back that confirms all essential details without broadcasting private information.
