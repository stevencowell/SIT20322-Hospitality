Task 1 · Lesson 4

Presentation and PPE

Prepare for kitchen work through hygienic personal presentation and choose, inspect and use personal protective equipment that matches the identified task hazard.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Links each presentation or PPE item to a real hazard
  • Distinguishes PPE from stronger workplace controls
  • Includes inspection and response to failure
  • Uses teacher-confirmed local requirements

Core ideas

Build the complete picture

1

Presentation is a safety and hygiene control

2

Clothing and footwear must suit the work

3

Control hair, nails, jewellery and personal items

4

Select PPE from the hazard and instructions

Visual explanation

Ready for the task, protected from the hazard

A neutral worker silhouette has callouts for controlled hair, clean clothing, hands, footwear and removable personal items. Beside it, a hazard-to-PPE matrix distinguishes heat, chemical, cutting and splash tasks and shows that process controls remain in front of PPE.

Worked example

Inspect, fit and use PPE correctly

Before use, check that the PPE is the correct type, clean or ready as required, undamaged and able to fit securely without restricting safe movement or visibility. Follow the demonstrated method for putting it on, using it, removing it and preventing contaminated surfaces from contacting skin, clothing or food.

Observed evidence: Before a fictional cleaning task, the authorised product information and teacher direction require splash protection, but the available eye protection has a cracked lens and will not sit securely. Decision and reason: The learner decides the PPE cannot provide the intended control and that beginning carefully would not compensate for damage. Safe action: They keep the item from use, leave the product closed and report the defect rather than modifying the PPE. Result: An approved replacement is provided and fitted using the demonstrated method before the task begins. Next check or record: The learner rechecks clothing, footwear, hair and task-specific PPE, then records the defective item and replacement without inventing a local sign-off process.

Question the room

Why is controlled hair part of kitchen readiness?

  1. It prevents hair entering food or equipment and reduces the need to touch it during preparation.
  2. It guarantees that no handwashing will be needed.
  3. It is only a visual style requirement for customers.

Apply

PPE and personal-readiness decisions

Four learners arrive for different teacher-directed kitchen tasks. Decide the next action without inventing the school's uniform or PPE rules.

  1. Select the strongest next action in each case.
  2. Check the consequence-based feedback.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why PPE is the final layer, not a repair for an unsafe process.
