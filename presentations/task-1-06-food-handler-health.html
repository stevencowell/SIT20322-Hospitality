Task 1 · Lesson 6

Food-handler health

Recognise when illness, symptoms, wounds or personal behaviour may affect safe food handling and report concerns early without recording private medical details.

Learning intention

What success looks like

  • Places protection and reporting before continued food handling
  • Addresses potentially affected food and equipment
  • Separates factual workplace information from private medical detail
  • Leaves duty and return decisions to the authorised person

Core ideas

Build the complete picture

1

Health can change the safety of a food-handling task

2

Report relevant symptoms promptly

3

Manage cuts and dressings as controlled items

4

Respond correctly to coughing, sneezing and face contact

Visual explanation

When health changes during food work

A decision flow begins with symptom, wound or respiratory event, then moves through Stop handling, Protect affected food, Report facts, Authorised duty decision and Return only when directed. A privacy shield diverts diagnoses and medical documents away from the learning site.

Worked example

Protect food when a health control fails

If illness, a wound or respiratory event may have exposed food, stop the affected task and keep the product from service. Tell the responsible person what occurred, which product or area may be affected and what immediate control was taken. Follow the authorised decision for disposal, reprocessing, cleaning and documentation.

Observed evidence: During a fictional preparation lesson, a learner develops a relevant illness symptom before beginning direct food handling. No food has yet been touched. Decision and reason: The learner does not decide privately that the symptom is minor because fitness for the task must be handled through the authorised health-reporting process. Safe action: They step away from the food zone, protect the untouched ingredients and speak privately to the responsible teacher. Result: The teacher determines suitable duties and any required controls; classmates receive no unnecessary health details. Next check or record: The learner confirms the new task boundary and records only the operational change and person notified, not a diagnosis or private medical information.

Question the room

A learner has symptoms that may affect safe food handling before class. What is the strongest response?

  1. Report the relevant concern before handling food and follow the responsible person's direction.
  2. Say nothing, wear gloves and complete the practical quickly.
  3. Begin preparation and report only if the food looks different.

Apply

PPE and personal-readiness decisions

Four learners arrive for different teacher-directed kitchen tasks. Decide the next action without inventing the school's uniform or PPE rules.

  1. Select the strongest next action in each case.
  2. Check the consequence-based feedback.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why PPE is the final layer, not a repair for an unsafe process.
